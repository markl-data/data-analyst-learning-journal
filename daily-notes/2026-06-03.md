## What I did Today
- Self-joins (table joined to itself)
- Anti-joins (LEFT JOIN + IS NULL, NOT EXISTS, NOT IN)

## Key concepts banked
- Self-join: same table, two aliases, useful for hierarchies and comparisons
- Anti-join (3 patterns): LEFT JOIN + IS NULL is the go-to
- Multi-table aggregation is "where real analyst SQL lives"

## What clicked
- Now naturally thinking in terms of group-level vs row-level logic, which is the biggest conceptual leap in SQL.

- Correctly used HAVING for group filters (D3, D4) without hesitation.

- Starting queries with the right mental model:
- “What level am I grouping at? What numbers do I need?”

- Consistently joining the right tables and grouping at the correct granularity.

- Beginning to see when a subquery is required (D2, D5).

## What's still awkward
- Row vs group comparisons (e.g., “biggest customer in a country”) still feel fuzzy - know a subquery is needed, but the structure isn’t automatic yet.

- Mixing aggregates and non-aggregates sometimes leads to grouping mistakes (e.g., grouping by product_name when you meant category).

- Profit logic (unit_price – unit_cost) vs revenue (i.amount) is still easy to mix up.

- COUNT vs SUM -Occasionally default to COUNT when the question needs SUM (e.g., quantity sold).

- Using aliases inside HAVING SQL doesn’t allow it.

- Subquery placement Good at writing them, but not yet confident about where they belong (SELECT vs WHERE vs HAVING).

## Open questions

- How do I structure a subquery that returns one value per group?  

- How do I compare a row to an aggregate of its group without window functions?  

- How do I avoid double-aggregating or averaging the wrong thing?  

- How do I confidently compute derived metrics like profit, margin, concentration, etc.?
