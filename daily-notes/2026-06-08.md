# 2026-06-08 - Day 8: Subqueries (Full Lesson)

## What I did today
- Three subquery placements formalised: WHERE, SELECT, FROM
- Correlated subqueries introduced (inner references outer's current row)
- EXISTS / NOT EXISTS pattern
- 18 exercises across Sets A, B, C, D, and synthesis

## What clicked
- Correlated subqueries finally felt natural - the “p1 row → p2 filter → return a category‑level value” pattern stopped feeling magical and started feeling mechanical.

- EXISTS vs. NOT EXISTS became clear: EXISTS = “at least one match”, NOT EXISTS = “no matches at all”, and both rely on the same correlated structure.

- Synthesis queries made more sense once I saw them as layered problems: compute X per group → compute an average of those X’s → compare.

## What was hardest
- S2’s multi‑layer logic - keeping “total revenue per customer” separate from “average of those totals” was mentally heavy; I kept trying to collapse them into one step.

- S5’s ‘closest without exceeding’ logic - juggling revenue per product, the average revenue, and then filtering + ranking required a level of structure I didn’t have at first.

- Remembering where aggregation is allowed - especially not mixing SUM/AVG inside WHERE without grouping first.

## Notes on duplication / readability
- Repeated subqueries showed up everywhere - especially repeating (SELECT SUM(amount) FROM invoices) or the same correlated AVG twice.

- Nested SELECT blocks got messy fast; I could feel the query getting harder to read even when the logic was correct.

- CTE‑shaped problems became obvious: any time I computed “revenue per X” and then needed that value again, I wished I could name it once and reuse it.
